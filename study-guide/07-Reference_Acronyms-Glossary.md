If a third-party source ever disagrees with an expansion here, CompTIA's official objectives PDF is the tiebreaker.

## Key term distinctions (understand, don't just memorize)

- **Threat vs. vulnerability vs. risk** — threat = the actor/event; vulnerability = the weakness; **risk = likelihood × impact** of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.
- **Inherent vs. residual vs. control risk** — before controls / after controls / risk that the controls themselves fail.
- **Risk appetite vs. tolerance** — org-wide strategic willingness vs. the acceptable variance around it.
- **Due diligence vs. due care** — investigate/assess beforehand vs. exercise ongoing reasonable protection.
- **Policy vs. standard vs. procedure vs. guideline** — intent (mandatory) / specific requirement (mandatory) / step-by-step (mandatory) / recommendation (optional).
- **MOU (Memorandum of Understanding) vs. MOA (Memorandum of Agreement)** — non-binding intent vs. binding formal responsibilities.
- **Vulnerability scan vs. penetration test** — identifies weaknesses vs. actively exploits to prove risk.
- **Known / partially known / unknown environment** — white / grey / black box (insider → outsider simulation).
- **Active vs. passive reconnaissance** — direct interaction (generates traffic, detectable) vs. OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence)/observation (no interaction).
- **IDS (Intrusion Detection System) vs. IPS (Intrusion Prevention System)** — alert only (out-of-band) vs. inline block.
- **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) vs. SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response) vs. XDR (Extended Detection and Response)** — aggregate/correlate/alert vs. automate/orchestrate response vs. cross-layer detection + response.
- **TPM (Trusted Platform Module) vs. HSM (Hardware Security Module) vs. TEE (Trusted Execution Environment)/secure enclave** — one device's motherboard chip / dedicated key appliance / protects data *in use*.
- **Tokenization vs. masking vs. hashing** — reversible via a vault / partial display / one-way.
- **Symmetric vs. asymmetric** — one shared key (bulk speed) vs. key pair (exchange, signatures, identity).
- **Hashing vs. encryption** — one-way integrity check vs. reversible confidentiality.
- **Digital signature keys** — sign with the sender's **private** key; verify with the sender's **public** key. Encrypt with the recipient's **public**; they decrypt with their **private**.
- **AH (Authentication Header (IPsec — integrity, no encryption)) vs. ESP** (IPsec) — integrity only vs. encryption + integrity (use ESP).
- **Transport vs. tunnel mode** (IPsec) — protect payload (host-to-host) vs. whole packet (gateway-to-gateway).
- **Full tunnel vs. split tunnel** VPN (Virtual Private Network) — all traffic through corp (secure, heavy) vs. only corp traffic (fast, riskier).
- **DAC (Discretionary Access Control) / MAC (Mandatory Access Control) / RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) / ABAC (Attribute-Based Access Control)** — owner decides / labels decide / role decides / attributes+context decide.
- **OAuth 2.0 vs. OIDC (OpenID Connect)** — delegated authorization vs. authentication (identity layer on top of OAuth).
- **SAML (Security Assertion Markup Language) vs. OIDC** — enterprise browser SSO (XML) vs. modern web/mobile (JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)/REST (Representational State Transfer)).
- **RTO (Recovery Time Objective) vs. RPO (Recovery Point Objective) vs. MTD (Maximum Tolerable Downtime)** — time to restore / tolerable data loss (in time) / absolute downtime limit. **RTO + WRT (Work Recovery Time) ≤ MTD.**
- **MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures) vs. MTTR (Mean Time To Repair / Recover)** — reliability (time between failures) vs. repair speed.
- **SLE (Single Loss Expectancy) vs. ALE (Annualized Loss Expectancy)** — one-event loss (`AV × EF`) vs. annual loss (`SLE × ARO`).
- **Preventive vs. deterrent** — actually stops it vs. only discourages the attempt.
- **Detective vs. corrective** — identifies/records vs. fixes/restores after.
- **Compensating control** — a substitute used *because the primary control isn't feasible* (not just "another control").
- **Data controller vs. processor** (GDPR) — decides why/how vs. acts on the controller's behalf.
- **Data owner vs. custodian/steward** — accountable, sets classification vs. day-to-day protection.
- **Typosquatting vs. pharming** — look-alike URL (Uniform Resource Locator) you clicked vs. correct URL redirected by poisoned DNS (Domain Name System).
- **Watering hole vs. supply chain** — compromise a site you visit vs. a vendor/component you use.
- **Whaling vs. BEC (Business Email Compromise)** — spear phishing *targeting* an exec vs. fraud *using* an exec's (spoofed/compromised) identity.
- **Worm vs. virus** — self-propagates, no user action vs. needs a host file and user action.
- **Password spraying vs. credential stuffing vs. brute force** — few common passwords across many accounts / reused breach creds / many guesses against one account.
- **Fail-open vs. fail-closed** — fails to *allow* (availability) vs. fails to *deny* (security).
- **Honeypot / honeynet / honeytoken** — one decoy host / decoy network / bait data or credential.
